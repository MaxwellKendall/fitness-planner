> frontal-plane hip-abduction/stability work (glute medius), not trunk flexion/rotation like plank.
> Recurring adductor strain is often linked to under-firing hip abductors forcing the adductors to
> do more frontal-plane stabilizing than they should; strengthening this side of the hip is a
> reasonable, low-risk complement to (not a substitute for) resolving the underlying issue.

## Cues
- Loop the band around the ankles (easier, more knee-dominant) or above the knees (lighter,
  more hip-dominant) — above the knees is the gentler starting position.
- Quarter-squat stance, knees soft and tracking over toes, chest up, hips back slightly.
- Step directly sideways, keeping tension on the band the entire time — never let the trailing
  leg fully relax or the feet drift together.
- Keep the toes pointed forward throughout; don't let the working hip rotate the foot outward.
- Take 8–12 steps one direction, then reverse — smaller, controlled steps beat big lunging ones.

## Programming Notes
Athlete's own addition, near-daily by request — reasonable for a light band-resistance accessory,
but "every day at real tension" risks becoming its own overuse issue. Recommended starting point:
- Light-to-medium band tension, above the knees (not ankles) to start.
- 2 sets of 10–15 steps per direction, most days of the week rather than strictly daily.
- This is a stability/activation exercise, not a strength PR chase — RPE should stay low (4–5),
  a burning sensation in the outer hip/glute is expected and fine.
- The return/eccentric phase does put some light tension through the adductors. Stop for the day
  if it produces a groin pull rather than the expected outer-hip burn — that's a different signal
  than normal training soreness, especially while the adductor issue is active.
- Not a substitute for addressing the underlying recurring strain (see profile.yaml `patterns` and
  the injury_prevention active goal) — a reasonable complement to run alongside it.

## Warm-up Protocol
No dedicated warm-up needed — this is itself a low-intensity activation exercise. Fine to use as a
warm-up for other lower-body work, or as a standalone daily addition on non-lifting days.
