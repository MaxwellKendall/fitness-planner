## Cues
- Rack position: bar rests on front delts, elbows driven high (upper arm near parallel to floor).
  Grip is either clean-grip (fingers under bar) or crossed-arm — use whichever keeps elbows up.
- More upright torso than a back squat — the front-loaded bar forces the chest to stay tall, which
  keeps the shin angle steeper and shifts load off the lower back relative to a back squat.
- Brace hard before unracking. Elbows up throughout — if elbows drop, the bar rolls forward.
- Sit down between the knees, not back. Depth: hip crease at or below top of knee.
- Drive up keeping the chest tall and elbows high the entire ascent.

## Programming Notes
Introduced 2026-07-25 as a lower-back-friendly alternative for reintroducing squat work during an
active lumbar pain watch (2 mentions logged: 07-14, 07-18). The more upright torso and shorter
moment arm on the lumbar spine make this a reasonable first squat variation to test before
returning to back squat. Treat as a brand-new lift, not a percentage of the athlete's back squat —
start conservative (RPE 6, 3–5 sets of 5) and let wrist/elbow/upper-back mobility (front-rack
position is often the limiting factor, not leg strength) guide loading before pushing higher.

## Warm-up Protocol
Hip circles × 10, front-rack mobility drill (empty bar, hold rack position × 5 breaths), bodyweight
squat × 10, then barbell: 1 × 8 @ empty bar, 1 × 5 @ ~50% working weight, then top sets.
