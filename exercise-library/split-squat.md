> **Adductor note:** listed here because it's unilateral, not because it avoids the adductors —
> unilateral squat-pattern work asks the working leg's adductors/abductors to stabilize the whole
> body alone, which is a real, non-trivial demand. Likely lower total stress than a heavy bilateral
> back squat (less load, more sagittal-plane), but not the "doesn't involve adductors" profile that
> the hinge movements (deadlift, DB RDL, good morning) have. Treat as still gated behind the same
> adductor caution as every other squat variant in this library, not as a clear substitute for it.

## Cues
- Feet split front-to-back, both facing forward (sagittal plane, not a lateral lunge). Torso upright.
- Lower straight down — front knee tracks over the front foot, back knee drops toward the floor.
- Both feet stay flat/stable throughout for the standard version (rear-foot-elevated is a harder
  variant with more front-leg hip flexion and stretch through the rear hip — not the starting point).
- Drive up through the front heel and midfoot. Keep the torso from pitching forward.
- Full range of motion should be pain-free before adding load — bodyweight first.

## Programming Notes
Unilateral squat-pattern option, listed as a substitute in this program for goblet squat and front
squat. Because each leg works independently, it doesn't let a stronger side compensate for a weaker
one the way a bilateral squat can — useful for addressing asymmetries, but also why it demands more
single-leg hip stability, not less, than a bilateral squat. Start bodyweight, 2–3 sets of 6–8/leg,
before adding any dumbbell load. Given the active right adductor/groin caution in this program,
treat any reintroduction of this lift the same as back/front/goblet squat: gate it behind a clean
movement check on a day with nothing else new in the session, rather than stacking it alongside
another fresh lower-body stimulus (e.g., not the same session hinge work is being reintroduced).

## Warm-up Protocol
Bodyweight hip circles × 10/side, bodyweight split squat × 5/leg (partial range, building to full),
then working sets.
