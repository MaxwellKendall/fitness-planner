> **Hip-hinge note:** this is performed from a bent-over stance — hips pushed back, torso ~45°,
> similar joint position to Romanian deadlift or good morning. It's a shorter, more static hold than
> a dynamic hinge lift (no repeated eccentric/concentric travel through the hip), but it is not
> hinge-free. If a hip-hinge pattern is under a constraint or caution (see config.yaml or the active
> plan), treat barbell row as covered by that caution too — dumbbell row (single-arm, one-hand/one-knee
> supported) or chest-supported row are lower hip-hinge-demand substitutes.

## Cues
- Grip just outside shoulder width, overhand. Hinge at the hips until torso is ~45° to the floor —
  soft knee bend, not a squat.
- Neutral spine throughout — no rounding under load. Brace the core before pulling.
- Pull the bar to the lower ribs/upper abdomen, elbows driving back and slightly up.
- Lower with control to full arm extension each rep — no bouncing the bar off the floor between reps
  unless deliberately resetting (dead-stop style).
- Keep the hips still — no using leg drive or torso swing to heave the weight up.

## Programming Notes
New lift as of 2026-08-21 — no working-weight history yet. Start conservative: empty bar for
technique, then a light working weight for the first several sessions before adding load. Because
the bent-over stance is a static hip-hinge hold, treat this the same as other hinge-pattern lifts for
recovery and injury-precaution purposes, even though the primary muscles trained are pull/back. Sets
of 6–10 are effective for back thickness; strict form matters more than load here.

## Warm-up Protocol
Scapular retractions (no weight) × 10, then empty bar × 10 (technique focus), then light working
weight × 8, then top sets.
