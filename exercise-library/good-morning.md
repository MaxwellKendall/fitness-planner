## Cues
- Bar sits in the low-bar squat position (rear delts, not the neck). Grip tight, elbows down.
- Soft knees, then hinge: push hips straight back until you feel a hamstring stretch. Torso to ~45°, no lower.
- Spine stays rigid-neutral the whole rep — brace before each rep like a heavy squat. The lift is a hinge, not a bow.
- Drive hips forward to stand. Squeeze glutes at the top; do not hyperextend the lumbar.
- If the low back rounds or the rep feels like lumbar flexion, the load is too heavy. This lift rewards patience.

## Programming Notes
Added 2026-07-16 as a lumbar-resilience accessory after a minor low-back tweak squatting
(athlete attributes it to erector weakness after time off). Purpose here is injury prevention,
not load PRs — the spinal erectors respond well to moderate reps at submaximal loads.

- Start with the empty bar (45 lb) × 8–10, RPE 5–6. Yes, really — technique demand is high
  and the lever arm on the lumbar is long.
- Progress +5–10 lb/week only when every rep is pain-free with a neutral spine.
- Program 1–2×/week, 2–3 sets of 8–12, on hinge days after the main pull or as a standalone accessory.
- Never grind a good morning. RPE ceiling 7, permanently.
- Hard stop if any lumbar pain during or after — swap to romanian-deadlift or back-extension and reassess.

## Warm-up Protocol
Bodyweight hip hinge drill × 10 (hands on hips, find the hamstring stretch),
then empty bar × 8 before any loaded sets.
